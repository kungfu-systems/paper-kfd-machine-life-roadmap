\begin{abstract}
Work on machine intelligence usually concentrates either on more capable
models or on anthropomorphic robots. This paper examines a different object:
a low-level machine organism assembled from ordinary servers, operating
systems, compilers, build tools, caches, gates, source code, executable
extensions, and replaceable reasoning agents. The proposal has two
load-bearing mechanisms. First, a KFD-shaped semantic kernel preserves
identity, direction, admitted facts, authority, and work continuity across
process and model replacement. Second, recursive dogfood lets the system
develop, qualify, admit, and use changes to its own executable organization.
We call this combination \emph{semantic autopoiesis}. The term denotes a
functional engineering hypothesis, not a claim of biological life,
sentience, or phenomenal consciousness.

We derive a minimal architecture in which scheduled heartbeats instantiate
fresh agents, verified work history restores relevant experience, KFX
extensions act as executable organs, and Buildchain-like qualification turns
candidate self-modifications into bounded successor states. We then examine a
dated Kungfu implementation snapshot. Native Initiative/Assignment work,
Episode and Project Cut evidence, Qualified Assignment Core, a verified Work
History Selector, KFX qualification, and recursive dogfood provide concrete
parts of the proposed loop; an open Product Release Cut boundary and the
absence of longitudinal survival trials prevent a claim that the organism
already exists as a completed system. Finally, we offer a staged roadmap and
falsifiable evaluations for continuity, self-maintenance, adaptation,
bounded autonomy, recovery, and human intervention. The central claim is
modest but consequential: current software engineering materials may already
be sufficient to construct and experimentally study a primitive form of
machine life before either general intelligence or humanoid embodiment is
available.
\end{abstract}
